# Excellence bar

Read this before a hunt and again before curation. A rare page is not enough. The blog has to be worth studying.

## What counts as a blog

A blog post is one article on a personal site, a lab blog, a small newsletter, or a practitioner blog. It has a byline, or it is obviously one person's site. It has a date, or you write `no date` after looking.

These are not blogs. Do not put them on the reading list:

- RFCs, vendor docs, OWASP pages, PortSwigger Academy, cheatsheets
- GitHub advisories, issues, and READMEs
- Awesome-lists, link roundups, and "complete guide" SEO posts
- Tweet threads with no article behind them
- Pages you did not fetch

Those may be leads. A lead is useful only when it names an author, a blog, or a post to open next.

## Two scores

Score both. Do not average them into one number.

| Stars | Rarity | Evidence you must record |
|---|---|---|
| ★5 | Almost unseen | Found from one blogroll, citation, or author search. A search for a distinctive sentence from the post returns almost nothing else. |
| ★4 | Niche | Not on the mainstream indexes you logged in the early rounds. Found from a person, not from a topic keyword on page 1. |
| ★3 | Known to specialists | Real post, but the big writeups already link it. |
| ★2 | Canonical | The post people already study. PortSwigger research, a famous researcher's index post, a vendor engineering blog. |
| ★1 | Generic | Drop it. |

"Feels rare" is not evidence. If you cannot say how you found it, it is ★2.

| Signal | Study value | What the fetched page must show |
|---|---|---|
| S5 | Changes how you study the corner | The author's own trace, failed attempt, or disagreement with the usual explanation. A learner can practice from it. |
| S4 | Worth a study session | Narrow, original, and specific. One corner, not a survey. |
| S3 | Correct, but replaceable | One extra detail on a post that already exists in a stronger form. |
| S2 | Intro or listicle | Defines the term, lists tips, or sells a tool. |
| S1 | No authorial work | SEO, an unsigned rewrite, or a page that only names the topic. |

S4 and S5 require all four of these, answered from the fetch, not from memory:

1. Mechanism or mistake. A trace, a failed attempt, or a wrong assumption.
2. Narrow. One corner. Not "the complete guide".
3. Original. The author's hunt, lab, review, or bug. Not a paraphrase of a cheatsheet.
4. Study hook. After reading, a learner can reproduce, compare, or question something. You do not write that lesson.

If the page does not show it, the score is S3 or lower.

## What to keep

A qualifying blog is ★4 or ★5, and S4 or S5.

Canonical posts (★2, S5) are anchors. Keep at most two, in their own section, so the learner knows what "mainstream" was. They do not fill the niche list.

Do not pad. Six real blogs beat fifteen weak ones.

## What "drives excellence" excludes

Do not keep a post because it is clever and obscure. Obscure and wrong wastes a study session. If the fetch does not show the author's work, drop it.

Do not keep a post because you can explain the topic from it. That is the old failure: research that turns into a lesson. The blog is the product. The lesson is Study-OS: <https://github.com/mccleod1290/Study-OS>
